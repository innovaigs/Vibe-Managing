# Vibe Managing

### Building Lovable for Business Management

---

## Thesis

Doing business is not easy. Managing a business is so hard that even though roughly 582 million people worldwide are business owners or actively starting one — about 7% of the global population — fewer than half of new businesses survive their first five years, and only one-third are still operating after a decade.[^1] [^2]

Out of all the business owners and entrepreneurs who do survive, the funnel of growth is brutal:

- Fewer than **5%** of all U.S. businesses ever reach **$1 million** in annual revenue.[^3]
- Of those that reach $1 million, fewer than **1%** of all businesses ever grow to **$10 million**.[^4]
- Of those that reach $10 million, only a fraction grow to **$100 million** — roughly **0.19%** of all U.S. businesses.[^5]
- Of those that reach $100 million, an even smaller fraction reach **$1 billion** — only about **5,100 companies** in the U.S. (roughly **0.03%** of all businesses).[^5]
- Companies that reach **$100 billion** in annual revenue? **Fewer than 30** exist worldwide.[^6]
- A company with **$1 trillion** in annual revenue? **Zero.** No company in history has ever crossed that threshold. The closest is Amazon, at approximately **$743 billion** in trailing-twelve-month revenue as of 2026.[^6]

Everyone can see and know how much it requires to make a business successful.

Is it not the same thing with tech? Is it not difficult to think, design, plan, write code, debug, maintain, and deploy a product? But AI has changed that. When Lovable launched on November 18, 2024, that day creating an app became like having a conversation with a person.[^7] [^8]

After thinking about this carefully, I asked myself a fundamental question: what problem was Lovable solving? It turns out the problem was to make creating an app as easy as possible for anybody. You don't need to understand coding. You don't need to understand what a database is. You don't need to understand how to deploy an app. Lovable solved the whole problem end to end — their focus was to make it possible for a non-technical person to build an app without having to understand what is going on under the hood.[^7] [^8]

**What if we can do the same for business?**

What if we can make it possible for someone to build a successful business without knowing and understanding what it means to manage a successful business?

As I was thinking about this, I asked myself the same questions Lovable's team asked themselves when they were solving this problem for non-technical people:

- How can we solve this end to end?
- Who else is already working on something like this?
- Is it really possible to solve this problem?
- What would it take to solve it?
- Will people pay for that?
- Is it a problem worth solving?

I have some answers for some of these questions. For some of them, I'm still looking. But the point is: why would it not be possible for someone to manage a business vibing — the same way the non-technical founder vibes and has fun building an app?

Doing business should not be this hard. It should not be only for a group of people; it should be available for anybody. I'm not saying everybody has the mental capacity to run a business — the same way not everybody can become a developer or an engineer. But the tools to do it should not be the barrier.

Is it a problem worth solving? Everyone would agree that this is a problem many companies have tried to solve, and people keep working to solve it. But the real question is: are they doing it the right way, with the right mindset? Do they want this to be solved end to end the same way Lovable solved building an app for the non-technical person?

There are already companies attempting pieces of this — Cogniver ("the first ERP you don't operate"), Jaisda ("the autonomous business operating system"), Neome (AI-powered business automation for SMEs), Vitelligence (AI-first business operations platform), and Lowco (a unified business operating system).[^9] Each is building part of the stack. But none has committed to solving it the way Lovable committed to solving app creation — end to end, for the person who doesn't want to understand what's under the hood.

I think this problem can be solved because the solutions for it are already out there, but not enough people care about solving it completely. Or maybe they think it would take too much to solve a problem like this. Or maybe they think it's unsolvable. Who knows.

But nothing can change my belief about this, because I know this is a real problem, I know it's worth solving, and I know it can get solved. That's why I decided to work on solving this problem myself.

Will I succeed? Yes. How do I know? Because I know that no matter what, the lessons I will learn by attempting to solve this will open new horizons for me and help me discover things I would not discover otherwise.

So I'm embarking on that journey of making managing a business as easy as using a vibe coding tool to build an app.
