# KNOWLEDGE_MAP

This is the intellectual index of the Vibe Managing business operating system — every useful business-management principle extracted from the source curriculum, de-branded, and restated as operational intelligence rather than course notes. It is organized by domain; each domain lists its core principles, the frameworks that drive decisions, the formulas/metrics with thresholds, representative IF/THEN decision rules, and the cross-domain links that make the whole system one connected engine. Use it as the reference an agent (or a founder) consults to answer "what business knowledge exists here, and what decision does it drive?"

## Strategy & Opportunity

Governs how the business is defined and how growth opportunities are discovered, screened, stress-tested, resourced, and de-risked before any capital is committed.

**Core principles**
- An "idea" is not an "opportunity" until it survives a defined pipeline (screen → statement → feasibility) — treat unvalidated ideas as hypotheses, not plans.
- There is no single "normal" growth curve; a business is always on one of four pathways (rapid / incremental / episodic / plateau), and the pathway determines whether today's problems are stage-normal or exceptional.
- Any threat is either a *risk* (known world, managed by data/forecasts) or an *uncertainty* (unknown world) — and the dangerous quadrant is low-probability/high-impact, which cannot be data-managed.
- Growth is gated by three factors ("3 Ms"): Management, Market, Money — the weakest one caps the plan.
- Every unanswered feasibility question is a data gap with an owner and a source, not a reason to stall.
- Founder-fit ("Fits me!") is a first-class gate, not an afterthought — a market-attractive opportunity that misfits the founder's life is flagged, not pursued.
- Social value and profitability reinforce each other ("doing well by doing good").

**Key frameworks**
- Idea-to-Growth-Plan Pipeline — go/refine/kill gating from raw idea to committed plan.
- Five Characteristics of a Good Opportunity — the pass/fail opportunity screen.
- Opportunity Statement (5 questions) — converts diagnosis into one EEC sentence.
- IdeaSpace (Grounded / Spaced-Out / Blue-Sky) — diverge then converge on ideas.
- Feasibility Assessment (6 dimensions) — stress-tests opportunity, customers, market, competition, financials, founder.
- Four Pathways to Growth — classifies the growth shape and its normal problems.
- Ways-to-Grow Taxonomy — the menu of growth levers (new markets/products/channels, partnerships, acquisitions).
- Competitive Mapping Grid — current vs. aspirational competitors + learn-more plan.
- Resource Map Gap Grid — six resource classes × have/need/action.
- Risk/Uncertainty 2×2 and Risk Audit Tool — classify then score, own, and instrument each risk.
- Crisis → Opportunity Reframe — extract new opportunity from a low-probability/high-impact event.
- Three-Lens Dashboard (YOU / BUSINESS / ENVIRONMENT) and EEC communication standard.

**Key formulas / metrics**

| Name | Formula / definition | Threshold / note |
|---|---|---|
| Risk score | integer 1–10 per risk | high (→10) requires contingency + owner + deadline + dashboard metric |
| Gross margin (opportunity) | (Price − COGS) / Price | illustrative 50% used in worked example, not a benchmark |
| Customer-acquisition ladder | plan first 5 → next 25 → next 100 | milestone structure, not a ratio |
| Market horizon set | history past 1/3/5 yrs; project 6mo, 1/2/3/5 yrs | required feasibility coverage |
| Survival base rates | ~1/2 of new firms survive 5 yrs; ~1/3 survive 10 yrs | expectation-setting, not a target |

**Key decision rules**
- IF an opportunity fails any of the Five Characteristics THEN send back to ideation / flag the specific weakness (no path to profit → do not advance to feasibility).
- IF a feasibility question can't be answered THEN log it as a data gap with a named source and assign it before committing resources.
- IF a threat is low-probability/high-impact THEN treat as potential crisis — insurance + contingency, not forecasting alone.
- IF sales are on a plateau THEN treat as reversible: test a strategy/tactics change or clear a technology/staffing/deployment roadblock.
- IF any of Management/Market/Money is inadequate THEN fix the weak factor before scaling.

**Cross-domain links**
- Resource Map "what I need" gaps → hiring (People), capital raise (Valuation), and process build (Operations).
- Risk Audit warning metrics → the Growth dashboard and monthly review alerts.
- Opportunity Statement + Feasibility → the Growth Plan's core (Growth & Execution) and the forecast's revenue assumptions (Finance).
- Growth pathway classification → which financial and operational strains to expect.

## Finance — Statements & Ratios

Governs constructing the three financial statements and reading them through ratios and benchmarks to answer: is the business profitable, efficient, well-financed, and improving vs. peers.

**Core principles**
- Profit ≠ cash: a firm can be profitable and cash-negative at once; sustainability needs BOTH positive net income and positive operating cash flow.
- Accrual timing accounts (A/R, A/P, inventory, prepaid, unearned, depreciation) are exactly where cash and income diverge.
- The Balance Sheet Equation (A = L + E) holds after every transaction — making it a construction engine for all three statements.
- ROE decomposes into three controllable levers (margin × turnover × leverage); always trace a ratio move back to the operating decision that caused it.
- A high ratio can be as bad as a low one (idle cash, missed free trade credit, over-leverage) — interpret against benchmarks, not in isolation.
- Analyze both time-series (one firm over years) and cross-sectional (vs. industry).

**Key frameworks**
- Three statements (IS/BS/SCF) and what each answers.
- Balance Sheet Equation as a transaction-to-statements engine.
- Category 1 (one-time/investing) vs. Category 2 (ongoing/operating) cash flows.
- DuPont decomposition and the ratio taxonomy tree (ROE → ROA → margin/turnover/leverage; liquidity in parallel).
- Common-size analysis; two comparison modes (time-series + benchmark).
- Burn rate & runway.

**Key formulas / metrics** (thresholds where stated)

| Metric | Formula | Healthy / signal |
|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | (Revenue − COGS) / Revenue | protect first; it funds all downstream costs |
| Operating (EBIT) margin | EBIT / Revenue | best cross-firm managerial measure |
| Net margin | Net Income / Revenue | positive & stable |
| ROA | Net Income / Total Assets | = margin × turnover |
| ROE | Net Income / Equity | = ROA × leverage; decompose before celebrating |
| Current ratio | CA / CL | ≥1.5 covenant-safe; 1.0 floor; <1.0 critical; too high = idle assets |
| Quick / acid-test | (CA − Inventory) / CL | ≥1.0 healthy; N/A for pure services |
| Times interest earned | EBIT / Interest | <~1.5 = financial stress |
| Times burden covered | EBIT / [Interest + Principal/(1−tax)] | tighter than TIE; covers interest + principal |
| Inventory turnover / days | COGS/Inventory ; 365/turnover | days rising vs. flat sales = obsolescence risk |
| A/R turnover / DSO | Credit Sales/A/R ; 365/turnover | DSO > terms granted = collection problem |
| A/P turnover / days | COGS/A/P ; 365/turnover | days >> supplier terms risks supply |
| Net operating (cash) cycle | Inv days + A/R days − A/P days | shorter = less working-capital need |
| Asset turnover | Revenue / Total Assets | sales per $ of assets |
| Fixed-asset turnover | Revenue / Net PP&E | watched by lenders in capital-intensive firms |
| Financial leverage | Total Assets / Equity | 1.0 = no debt; magnifies ROA up and down |
| Interest-bearing D/E | IB debt / Equity | rising = risk |
| Revenue growth % | (Curr − Prior)/Prior | growth burning cash = warning |

Construction identities: Gross Profit = Rev − COGS; EBIT = Gross − Opex; EBT = EBIT − Interest; Net Income = EBT − Tax; EBITDA = EBIT + D&A; Ending R/E = Beginning R/E + NI − dividends; Operating CF = NI + Dep ± ΔWC.

**Key decision rules**
- IF a loan covenant sets current ratio ≥1.5 AND actual < 1.5 THEN loan is technically in default — remediate before breach (risk of acceleration/collateral).
- IF gross margin declines THEN diagnose price cuts / input inflation / mix shift before touching opex; IF operating margin falls faster than gross THEN the problem is an opex line (find it via common-size).
- IF ROE is high THEN decompose with DuPont to confirm it isn't just leverage.
- IF a debt-funded project's return < the debt's interest rate THEN it destroys owner value — don't fund with debt.
- IF a forecast's assets ≠ liabilities + equity THEN the gap is the funding needed — close via the remediation stack or raise capital.

**Cross-domain links**
- Ratios + benchmarks feed Valuation (margin story, leverage, bankability) and the Growth dashboard (financial-health KPIs).
- Working-capital days connect directly to Operations (inventory, order-to-cash processes) and to the cash forecast.
- Covenant thresholds connect to financing decisions and risk warning metrics.

## Finance — Forecasting & Cash

Governs building linked driver-based forecasts, managing cash and working capital, running scenarios, and diagnosing profit-vs-cash divergence.

**Core principles**
- A forecast is a small documented assumption set that drives a fully linked three-statement model; the balance sheet's imbalance IS the funding needed.
- Model a growth opportunity *incrementally* on top of the existing business, then combine — never re-forecast the whole company from scratch.
- Cash flow (not profit) is the survival test; the single most important question is "is operating cash flow positive?"
- Interest depends on the debt balance, which depends on the cash plug — the model resolves this circularity iteratively.
- Prefer scenario analysis (coherent bundles of interrelated assumptions) over single-variable sensitivity, because real assumptions move together.

**Key frameworks**
- Driver-based 3-statement forecast (9 layers: base → revenue → COGS → opex → depreciation → EBIT/below-line → working-capital drivers → fixed assets → financing/cash plug → SCF).
- Opportunity/new-venture quarterly forecast (volume ramp → break-even/ROI/payback).
- BSE transaction engine; Category 1 vs. 2 cash flows; DuPont; common-size; ratio pyramid.
- Minimum-cash + revolver plug logic (draw below minimum, pay down above).

**Key formulas / metrics**

| Name | Formula | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Burn / net burn | cash opex (− revenue) per period | falling toward breakeven = healthy |
| Runway | Cash ÷ burn rate | >12mo healthy / 6–12 warning / <6 critical |
| Working-capital balances | A/R=(Rev/365)×DSO; Inv=(COGS/365)×DIO; A/P=(COGS/365)×DPO | forecast drivers |
| Contribution margin/unit | Price × (1 − COGS%) | basis for break-even |
| Break-even units | Fixed costs ÷ contribution/unit | actual comfortably above BE |
| Initial investment | Capex + net incremental working capital | for opportunity go/no-go |
| ROI (opportunity) | Steady-state operating profit ÷ initial investment | worked ex. ~331% |
| Straight-line depreciation | Asset cost ÷ useful life | non-cash; add back in CF |
| Fully-loaded wage | Base × (1 + benefits/payroll-tax %) | worked ex. +25% |
| Ending cash | Beginning + Operating + Investing + Financing CF | drives revolver logic |

**Key decision rules**
- IF projected cash < minimum balance THEN draw the line of credit (up to cap); IF the draw exceeds the cap THEN flag a financing gap.
- IF operating cash flow is negative for multiple periods THEN a capital infusion is coming — surface it early.
- IF net income positive but operating CF negative THEN investigate rising A/R, inventory build-up, low A/P before assuming health.
- IF COGS is expected to fall with scale THEN model COGS% declining rather than flat.
- IF an assumption is material and uncertain THEN run base/optimistic/pessimistic scenarios and name the assumption the plan is most fragile to.

**Cross-domain links**
- Consumes Strategy's opportunity/feasibility assumptions; feeds Valuation (EBITDA, DSCR) and Growth & Execution (the 5-year forecast and monthly variance loop).
- Working-capital levers connect to Operations (order-to-cash, inventory) and to bankability (Valuation).

## Valuation & Money

Governs what the business is worth, which levers raise that worth, how to finance growth, and how to prove creditworthiness and prepare for exit.

**Core principles**
- Value = what a buyer will pay at a point in time; it moves with economic conditions and financing availability.
- Assets with similar value drivers sell for similar prices — value by comparable multiples, triangulated across revenue/EBITDA/net income.
- EBITDA multiple is the default primary method for small businesses (closest simple cash-flow proxy); revenue and net-income multiples cross-check it.
- Intangibles (loyal customers, committed team) drive value indirectly by producing more revenue/profit/cash flow.
- Bankability = describe growth in financial terms AND show a credible repayment story; funders always want a forecast.
- Match funding to need: short-term need → short-term instrument; long-term asset/growth → term loan or equity.

**Key frameworks**
- Relative (comparable/multiples) valuation, 3-step; value-driver selection.
- Financing decision framework (debt vs. equity vs. line vs. grant vs. self-fund).
- Bankability / 5 C's of Credit; financing pitch model; exit-readiness checklist.

**Key formulas / metrics**

| Metric | Formula | Threshold / benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue multiple value | Revenue × avg(Sale Price/Revenue) | small biz ~0.5–1.5× typical |
| EBITDA multiple value | EBITDA × avg(Sale Price/EBITDA) | small biz ~3–7× (primary method) |
| Net-income multiple value | Net Income × avg(Sale Price/NI) | worked comps ~9–15× |
| Indicated value | average of the per-multiple estimates (report range) | large gaps = margin story to investigate |
| DSCR | Operating Profit ÷ Debt Payment | ≥1.25 min; 1.5–3.0 strong |
| Owner equity contribution | Equity ÷ project cost | ~10–25% supports bankability |
| SDE | NI + owner comp + interest + tax + D&A + one-offs | micro-business valuation basis |
| Professional valuation cost | — | ~$4k–8k for a real transaction |

**Key decision rules**
- IF valuing a small business THEN default to EBITDA multiple, cross-check revenue and net-income.
- IF revenue-multiple estimate >> EBITDA-multiple estimate THEN margins are below comps — fix margins before selling.
- IF significant future growth (signed contracts) is not in trailing financials THEN add forward value (DCF/adjustment) with evidence.
- IF DSCR < ~1.25 under the funded plan THEN not bankable as debt — shrink the loan, extend term, add equity, or improve the plan.
- IF need is short-term/seasonal THEN use a line of credit, not a term loan or equity.

**Cross-domain links**
- Consumes Finance (EBITDA, margins, forecast, DSCR) and Strategy value-drivers; feeds Growth & Execution (exit tracking) and financing initiatives.
- Value-destroyers (customer concentration, owner-dependence, high leverage) tie back to People (delegation), Marketing (segment mix), and Finance (deleveraging).

## Leadership

Governs the founder's default style, self-awareness, adapting to the people they lead, developing as a leader, and setting mission/vision.

**Core principles**
- Every leader is a mix of four styles and defaults to one; effectiveness comes from *flexing* to another when the default isn't working.
- Under stress each style degrades into a predictable backup behavior — detecting it is the signal to reduce load and re-flex.
- Observable style sits on inner drivers: Identity → Motives → Values → Beliefs; beliefs about human nature directly determine delegation behavior.
- Compassion works only when combined with understanding others' motivations and the discipline to deliver on priorities; it reads as stronger, not softer.
- A good leader gets people to follow because they *want to* — prefer influence and buy-in over coercion.

**Key frameworks**
- Two-dimension style model: Assertiveness × Responsiveness → Driver / Expressive / Amiable / Analytical.
- Style-shift-under-stress: Analytical→Avoiding, Driver→Dictatorial, Amiable→Accommodating, Expressive→Attacking.
- "What shapes your leadership" (Identity/Motives/Values/Beliefs); compassionate leadership.
- Mission (NOW/DO) → Vision (FUTURE/BECOME), bridged by Growth Plan (Strategy → Objectives → Tactics → Metrics).

**Key formulas / metrics**
- Style scoring: mean of each rating battery (1.0–4.0); midline threshold 2.5 → More/Less on each axis; |score − 2.5| < 0.4 flags a blended/near-midline style.
- Leadership growth: the founder defines **three** personal growth metrics (e.g., % of key employees whose non-pay motivators the founder can name; share of decisions made without the founder; coaching/feedback cadence).

**Key decision rules**
- IF the other person is a Driver/Expressive/Amiable/Analytical THEN flex accordingly (Driver → be brief/results; Expressive → big-picture/recognition; Amiable → rapport/reassurance; Analytical → data/time/process).
- IF the founder shows a backup behavior THEN identify the stressed style and de-escalate (restore delegation for a stressed Driver, set a decision deadline for a stressed Analytical, etc.).
- IF you cannot state why an employee works for you beyond pay THEN ask them directly — do not assume.
- IF control-oriented beliefs dominate THEN the founder is a delegation risk — recommend authority-delegation work.

**Cross-domain links**
- Delegation-readiness beliefs → People & Org (levels of authority, founder-as-bottleneck).
- Mission/vision → Growth & Execution (vision plan, leadership section of the growth plan) and Marketing (purpose/positioning).
- Founder motives → Valuation (exit intent) and Strategy (aspirations, founder-fit gate).

## People & Org

Governs hiring and selection, behavioral interviewing, delegation and authority, onboarding, and treating the organization as a system.

**Core principles**
- The org is a system: every hire changes it — anticipate the ripple before hiring.
- Past behavior is the best predictor of future behavior — interview for what candidates *did*, never hypotheticals ("stay out of the woulds").
- "Fit" means alignment to mission/values/working-approach and a complementary perspective — NOT personal similarity (that's affinity bias → homogeneity).
- Delegate the *ends*, leave the *means* to the employee unless the method itself is critical (safety/compliance/brand).
- Grant authority per task-type as competence is proven; the target state is Level D (complete authority) on most tasks, which buys back the founder's time.

**Key frameworks**
- HR lifecycle: Recruit → Interview → Hire → Onboard → Evaluate → Delegate → Develop → Thrive/Promote/Separate (culture envelops all).
- Selection model (visible / intangible / company-alignment); cultural-fit (correct vs. affinity-bias).
- Behavioral interviewing ladder (per critical task); recruiting lenses (purposeful careers / tight market / bias check).
- Delegation setup (5 elements) + Levels of Authority A–D; three instruction types (orders / requests / suggestions).
- Organization-as-a-system model.

**Key formulas / metrics**
- Employee fit score (1–5) and development-potential score (1–5) per key employee.
- Delegation depth (share of tasks at Level C/D vs. A/B); founder discretionary time reclaimed.
- Standard hiring metrics: cost per hire, time to fill, time to productivity, offer-accept rate, quality-of-hire (90/180-day retention/performance), turnover/retention %.
- Onboarding completion within 3 days of start; HR-process coverage vs. the standard procedure list.
- Interview scoring: A–D per question; overall Highly Recommend / Acceptable / Acceptable-with-Reservations / Unacceptable.

**Key decision rules**
- IF task is high-importance/difficult/novel OR employee is new THEN Level A + Orders; IF competence proven THEN Level D + Suggestions.
- IF the founder is the single point of failure on a task a competent employee could own THEN delegate at the highest justified level rather than doing it.
- IF a proposed interview question isn't clearly job-related or touches a protected class THEN drop it and route to legal.
- IF "fit" reasoning rests on shared background/comfort THEN reject as affinity bias; re-score against mission/values.
- IF considering termination/reclassification THEN follow documented procedure and require legal counsel.

**Cross-domain links**
- Delegation and founder-capacity → Leadership (beliefs), Operations (process ownership), and cash (hiring is a cash decision).
- Org-development gap plan → Strategy resource map and Growth & Execution hiring roadmap.
- Interviewing/classification guardrails → Legal & Risk escalation.

## Marketing & Customer

Governs who the customer is, why they buy, the value proposition, and the channels/tactics that move them from awareness to advocacy.

**Core principles**
- Strategy before tactics: decide *who* and *what value* before *how to reach* and *how to convert*.
- Segment, then serve — market to distinct segments differently (separate persona, CVP, and funnel per segment with different buying behavior).
- The marketing funnel is the seller's mirror of the buyer's journey; define a Message, Tool, and Action at every stage.
- A CVP is an internal decision rationale for why to buy from you vs. competitors — not a tagline or slogan.
- On social, serve identity/info/emotion — "no one is looking to shop"; obey the 80/20 and 3:1 content ratios.
- Prioritize reach/impressions and traffic/conversions over vanity likes; align every metric to a business objective.

**Key frameworks**
- Marketing process chain; segmentation (demographic/geographic/behavioral/psychographic); MAAW segment screen (Measurable/Accessible/Actionable/Worthwhile).
- Customer persona (4 quadrants); buying center (6 roles); buyer's journey (5 stages, +spec/RFP for B2B/B2G).
- Competitive-advantage matrix; CVP formula + 4 elements + gauge; elements of influence (6 persuasion levers per stage).
- Digital: POEM (paid/owned/earned); paid-vs-organic matrix; conversion-centered design (4 C's); EEAT; search intent; social motivations & 3 pillars.

**Key formulas / metrics**

| Metric | Formula | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Post engagement rate | engagements ÷ impressions | higher better |
| Click-through rate | clicks ÷ impressions | creative/offer strength |
| Reach | followers + sharers' followers | potential unique viewers |
| Social conversions | purchases/leads from social visitors | the true ROI metric |
| Revenue mix by segment | segment revenue ÷ total revenue | concentration risk if one dominates |
| 80/20 rule | ≤20% promotional posts | content balance |
| 3:1 ratio | 3 non-promo : 1 promo | content calendar |
| Conversion rate (per stage) | # advancing ÷ # entering | *added to complete domain* |
| CAC / LTV / LTV:CAC | spend÷new customers ; AOV×freq×lifespan(×margin) ; ≥3:1 healthy | *added to complete domain* |
| ROAS | ad revenue ÷ ad spend | *added to complete domain* |

**Key decision rules**
- IF a segment fails any of MAAW THEN don't target it as primary — re-segment or deprioritize.
- IF the CVP hits only 2–3 of the 4 elements THEN it is weak — revise until all four are present and specific.
- IF customer is at Awareness/Interest/Desire/Action/Retention THEN apply the stage's message-tool-action + influence lever (e.g., Interest → lead-capture asset + social proof; Action → remove friction + scarcity).
- IF targeting NEW customers THEN run paid look-alike awareness; IF nurturing EXISTING THEN run organic mid-funnel for engagement/referrals.
- IF a homepage scores red/yellow on any 4 C THEN fix it before spending on traffic.

**Cross-domain links**
- Personas/segments → Strategy (opportunity, customer validation) and revenue-mix concentration risk (Finance/Valuation).
- Funnel volume assumptions → the revenue forecast (Finance) and pipeline KPIs (Growth & Execution).
- Competitive advantage → Valuation (defensible model, premium multiple) and Strategy (differentiation).

## Sales & Negotiation

Governs claiming and creating value in any deal — pricing, terms, contracts, partnerships, and supplier negotiations — through disciplined preparation.

**Core principles**
- Negotiate on interests (the "why"), not positions (the stated demand).
- Know your BATNA and estimate theirs; a strong BATNA is leverage — use it to warn, not threaten.
- Claim value when the pie is fixed/one-time; create value (expand the pie) when the relationship or repeat business matters.
- Never concede unilaterally — counter a concession demand with a concession ask (bilateral).
- Don't discuss price until you understand their interests; don't anchor solely on price — offer good/better/best packages.

**Key frameworks**
- Negotiation prep framework: Target / Limit (reservation) / ZOPA / BATNA / Interests / Options / Standards / Resources / Relationship / Communication plan.
- Claiming (distributive) vs. creating (integrative) value; at-the-table tactics; top-10 high-stakes strategies.
- Elements of influence (shared with Marketing): reciprocity, consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity.

**Key formulas / metrics**
- ZOPA exists IF the parties' limits overlap; if not, add interests/resources/options to build a "New ZOPA."
- Candidate KPIs: % reaching agreement; % of outcomes ≥ target; value captured within ZOPA (outcome vs. limit); concessions given-vs-received ratio; prep-sheet completion rate before negotiating.

**Key decision rules**
- IF you don't yet understand the other side's interests THEN do NOT discuss price.
- IF asked to concede THEN propose a bilateral concession (get something back).
- IF no ZOPA on price alone THEN add interests/resources/options before walking.
- IF the deal is one-time/distributive THEN claim value; IF relationship/repeat matters THEN create value.

**Cross-domain links**
- Standards/benchmarks draw on Finance and Marketing data; deal terms and contracts route to Legal & Risk.
- Pricing outcomes flow into the revenue forecast (Finance) and margin/value story (Valuation).
- Influence levers are shared with the Marketing funnel.

## Operations

Governs the value-creation engine: how inputs become outputs, where processes break, how to scale them, and what to automate, document, delegate, or outsource.

**Core principles**
- Every operation is Input → Transformation → Output; every transformation either creates or destroys value — there is no neutral step.
- Diagnose in order: Effectiveness (does it work?) → Efficiency (least waste?) → Competitiveness (efficiently producing what customers value?).
- A process must be Understood → Rationalized → Codified → Monitored to support growth; undocumented = un-delegable = a scaling ceiling.
- The bottleneck (slowest step) sets the whole process's capacity — fix it before optimizing anything else.
- Start technology decisions from the burning need, not the tool; only the right tool at the right price for *this* business.

**Key frameworks**
- Operations system (input–transform–output); effectiveness/efficiency/competitiveness triad.
- Scope-of-operations checklist; process lifecycle for growth (understand→rationalize→codify→monitor).
- Process mapping method (sub-processes + details; points of failure; recovery; fail-safe/poka-yoke).
- Operational audit (5 dimensions: overview / finance ops / organizational / market-value / metrics).
- Automate/document/delegate/outsource routing.

**Key formulas / metrics**

| KPI | Formula | Healthy / Warning / Critical |
|---|---|---|
| Cycle time | start→output per unit | at/below target / 10–25% over / >25% over |
| Process cycle efficiency | VA time ÷ total cycle time | >25% / 10–25% / <10% |
| Throughput | units ÷ time (or WIP ÷ cycle time) | ≥ demand / 90–99% / <90% |
| Capacity utilization | actual ÷ max | 70–85% / 85–95% or <60% / >95% or <40% |
| On-time delivery | on-time ÷ total | ≥95% / 85–95% / <85% |
| Defect rate | defects ÷ total | <2% / 2–5% / >5% |
| First-pass yield | clean ÷ started | >95% / 85–95% / <85% |
| Process doc coverage | documented ÷ key processes | 100% / 60–99% / <60% |
| Automation ROI | (annual manual cost saved − tool cost) ÷ tool cost | positive & fast payback |

Supporting: takt time = available time ÷ demand; hand-off count as a delay/error proxy; labor utilization = productive ÷ available hours.

**Key decision rules**
- IF a key process is undocumented THEN codify it (write the SOP) before delegating or scaling.
- IF a step is non-value-added THEN eliminate it; if it can't be eliminated, minimize or automate.
- IF a step is repetitive/rule-based/high-volume/low-judgment AND a right-fit affordable tool exists THEN automate.
- IF an activity is non-core and low customer-visible value THEN evaluate outsourcing/buying.
- IF a part of operations strains most as sales rise THEN treat it as the primary scaling constraint and resource it ahead of growth.

**Cross-domain links**
- Process ownership → People & Org (delegation, authority levels) and Leadership (founder-as-bottleneck).
- Working-capital processes (billing/collection, inventory) → Finance cash cycle.
- Scaling constraints → Strategy (feasibility "what strains under load") and Growth & Execution (process-evolution roadmap).

## Growth & Execution

Governs assembling the growth plan, generating and prioritizing initiatives, building the monitoring dashboard, and running the forecast-vs-actual learning loop.

**Core principles**
- The growth plan is one living document; the Executive Summary is written LAST and the dashboard is updated continuously as each section deposits metrics.
- Growth options come from four sources: resource gaps, the opportunity statement, market trends, and risk-mitigation steps.
- An initiative without an owner, a deadline, and a warning-signal metric is not execution-ready — block it.
- Gate on the binding constraint first (capital → hiring → systems → contracts unblock everything else).
- Monitor across three domains — YOU (founder), YOUR BUSINESS (internal), YOUR ENVIRONMENT (external) — mixing leading and lagging indicators.
- The learning loop is COMPARE → CLASSIFY → DIAGNOSE → ADAPT → NEXT ACTION; never run variance analysis on half-populated data.
- A favorable single-metric variance can be an artifact (e.g., low expenses because activity stalled) — interpret against co-moving metrics.

**Key frameworks**
- Growth-plan template (12 sections) + the 3-slide/3-minute growth-plan pitch.
- Growth-strategy generation (resource/opportunity/market/risk-driven) + strategic pathways menu (value-chain repositioning, offer expansion, recurring-revenue shift, verticalization, hybrid human+tech, etc.).
- Initiative prioritization (impact/effort/cost/risk/dependency/founder-fit).
- Dashboard structure (YOU/BUSINESS/ENVIRONMENT; leading vs. lagging; risk warning-signals).
- Forecast-vs-actual monthly review loop.

**Key formulas / metrics**

| Name | Formula | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Variance amount / % | Actual − Forecast ; (Actual−Forecast)/Forecast | divide-by-zero when Forecast=0 → "no comparison" |
| Growth rate / CAGR | (Vt−Vt-1)/Vt-1 ; (End/Begin)^(1/yrs)−1 | trend measures |
| Rule of 40 | Revenue growth % + Net profit margin % | target ≥40 |
| LTV:CAC | (contract value × retention × margin) ÷ CAC | ≥3:1 healthy |
| Net revenue retention | (retained + expansion) ÷ starting revenue | expansion health |
| Cash runway | Cash ÷ monthly burn | <6 mo triggers funding priority |
| Days to get paid / pay | A/R ÷ (Rev/day) ; A/P ÷ (Costs/day) | monitored monthly |
| Expected initiative impact | Σ(prob of success × forecast contribution) | ranks initiatives |

Monitored monthly with three baselines each: vs Forecast, vs Previous Period, vs Previous Year; plus "Top Movers" attribution.

**Key decision rules**
- IF forecast OR actual is missing for the month THEN block the review and surface the data gap.
- IF expenses below forecast AND revenue also below forecast THEN treat as stalled activity, not savings.
- IF the same assumption misses direction 2+ months THEN re-forecast it (systematic, not noise).
- IF cash runway < 6 months THEN make funding/cash-preservation the top-priority initiative.
- IF a risk's warning-signal crosses its threshold THEN activate its contingency plan and notify the owner.
- IF an initiative lacks owner/deadline/metric THEN block it from the active plan.

**Cross-domain links**
- Integrates every other domain: opportunity/feasibility/risk (Strategy), forecast (Finance), leadership/vision (Leadership), org plan (People), marketing plan (Marketing), operational plan (Operations), bankability/exit (Valuation).
- Risk warning-signals originate in Strategy's Risk Audit; dashboard financial KPIs originate in Finance.

## Legal & Risk

Governs employment-law compliance, worker classification, restrictive covenants, IP/trade-secret protection, contracts, and when to escalate to counsel. All content is framing guidance, not legal advice.

**Core principles**
- This layer surfaces issues and escalation triggers — it never renders legal advice; route binding decisions to a qualified attorney, and re-verify thresholds by jurisdiction and year.
- More laws apply as headcount grows — re-run compliance review at every hiring milestone.
- Worker misclassification carries heavy exposure and the burden of proof is on the employer — don't default to "contractor" for cost reasons.
- Trade-secret protection is a layered program (NDAs + handbook + need-to-know access + fast enforcement), not one document.
- A remote worker is governed by the employment law where they *live*, not where the business sits.

**Key frameworks**
- Employment-law headcount thresholds table (e.g., anti-discrimination 15+, ADEA 20+, FMLA 50+/75-mi, FLSA 1+); prohibitions/obligations (harassment, retaliation, accommodation).
- Worker classification: Right-to-Control test + Economic-Realities test.
- Restrictive covenants (non-compete / non-solicit / NDA); trade-secret/IP protection program; remote-workforce risk; interview/hiring hygiene; employee handbook.
- Entity-structure baseline (sole prop / partnership / LLC / S-corp / C-corp) and commercial-contract essentials.
- Negotiation prep framework (shared with Sales & Negotiation).

**Key formulas / metrics** (candidate)
- % of workers with signed agreements + IP-assignment; % of contracts reviewed against the pre-signature checklist before signing; contract cycle time (draft→signed); # open compliance gaps by severity; # of items escalated to counsel and their resolution time.

**Key decision rules**
- IF the company controls the manner/means/product of the work THEN treat the worker as an EMPLOYEE (or escalate).
- IF headcount crosses a threshold THEN trigger compliance review + attorney check.
- IF drafting/enforcing any restrictive covenant THEN require attorney review (jurisdiction-specific enforceability).
- IF any escalation trigger is present (binding contract, entity change, hire/fire/reclassify, discrimination/harassment/retaliation signal, accommodation request, suspected IP theft, cross-state/remote hire, litigation/audit/subpoena, IP filing) THEN STOP and route to counsel.
- IF in doubt THEN frame the question and escalate — never generate content that reads as a final legal position.

**Cross-domain links**
- Hiring/classification/termination → People & Org; contract terms → Sales & Negotiation and Valuation (financing docs, personal guarantees).
- Compliance and IP-protection gaps → Strategy's Risk Audit and Growth dashboard warning metrics.
- Remote/multi-state operations → Finance (tax/withholding) and Operations (documentation).

## Cross-cutting themes

A handful of principles recur across nearly every domain — they are the system's spine:

- **Cash is survival.** Profit ≠ cash; operating cash flow, burn, and runway gate every decision (Finance, Forecasting, Valuation, Growth). "Happiness is a positive operating cash flow."
- **Concentration is risk.** Customer concentration, single-supplier dependence, thin equity, and one-channel reliance all lower value and raise fragility (Marketing revenue-mix, Valuation value-destroyers, Finance leverage, Strategy risk).
- **Founder-as-bottleneck.** The founder is the scarce resource; delegation to Level D, documented processes, and hiring buy back discretionary time (Leadership, People, Operations, Growth).
- **Idea vs. validated opportunity.** Nothing advances on assertion — screen, state, and stress-test before committing (Strategy, Finance forecasting, Growth feasibility gates).
- **Measure leading + lagging, and instrument every risk.** Every metric needs a purpose, source, and decision it drives; every top risk needs a warning-signal threshold on the dashboard (Strategy, Finance, Operations, Growth).
- **Interpret in context, never in isolation.** A high ratio, a favorable variance, or a low expense can each be bad — read against benchmarks, terms, and co-moving metrics (Finance, Operations, Growth).
- **Fit and alignment over similarity/comfort.** Hire, partner, and pursue opportunities for alignment to goals/values, not personal resemblance (People affinity-bias, Strategy founder-fit, Leadership).
- **Codify to scale.** Undocumented knowledge (process, assumption, agreement) is un-delegable and un-defensible (Operations SOPs, Legal agreements, Finance assumption sets).
- **Interests over positions; create value where relationships matter.** Applies to negotiation and, by extension, to partnerships, hiring, and customer relationships (Sales, Marketing influence, People).
- **Escalate irreversibles.** Legal, financial-commitment, and high-impact/low-probability decisions get a human/attorney gate — the system frames, it does not bind (Legal, Strategy risk/uncertainty).

A smaller share is **SYNTHESIZED** (reusable patterns generalized from worked examples, or standard-practice thresholds added to make a source concept executable), and a modest share is **CLAUDE-DERIVED** (domain knowledge added to fill acknowledged curriculum gaps).
